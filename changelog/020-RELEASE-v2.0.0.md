# v2.0.0 — the world rebuilt (2026-08-20)

The largest release in AresRPG's history: the whole game was rebuilt from its foundations. A new
world engine, a new interface, and live multiplayer — everything you see, walk on, and fight in
was made again to be faster, cleaner, and truer to the chain.

## A new world

- **A rebuilt world engine.** Terrain, biomes, water, sky, and light are generated and rendered
  by an entirely new engine — day and night roll over procedural landscapes with structures to
  find and far more world on screen at once.
- **A living map.** A compass strip reads your heading, a minimap in the corner tracks your
  position live, and discovering a zone reveals its monsters and resources — including a full
  world map of everywhere you have been.
- **You come back where you left.** Log out anywhere; the game resumes you there, reconciled
  against your last on-chain checkpoint.

## Playing together

- **Other players are THERE.** See everyone around you move through the world in real time, with
  their own colors, cosmetics, and mounts — riders sit their pets, runners run, and whoever
  arrives last still sees everyone instantly.
- **World chat.** Talk with everyone in your world; your own lines stand out in blue. Party chat
  and whispers ride along.
- **Fights, reconnected.** The deterministic turn-based combat drives the new engine end to end —
  casts, buffs, displacement, and deaths all animate from the chain's own truth, and casting on
  yourself finally looks like a buff instead of a swing.

## Getting started

- **Character creation guides you.** Pick a class, colors, and a name with a live 3D preview —
  and if your wallet can't cover the creation cost yet, the game says exactly what you need and
  where to send it before you commit anything.
- **The game keeps itself fresh.** New versions reach your open tab on their own — no more stale
  clients, and the version you're running is visible right in the sidebar.

One highlighted fix: moving around no longer risks a wrongful disconnect after a connection
hiccup — the movement check now understands network stalls instead of reading them as cheating.
